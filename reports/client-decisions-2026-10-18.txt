4. COUNTER SCHEME
One-based numbering. A form says Step n of N; an ongoing case says Stage n of N.
Payment/success are outside intake numbering. Review screens inside an intake
count as a step. Subprocesses (delivery, manual verification, fee requests) have
separate, explicitly scoped counters; they do not reset the main case stages.
Changed flows:
- Legal Advice intake: five steps; review is step 5, not a sixth step.
- FIR status/ready: the same five lifecycle stages on both screens; status 2/5,
  ready 5/5. FIR's existing five-step intake stays five.
- Advocate enrollment: Personal, Professional, Bar Council, Practice,
  Documents, Review — steps 1–6. Incomplete documents return to step 5.
- District Court intake: Case Type, Details, Documents, Service, Review — 1–5.
- High Court new-case intake: Case Type, Matter Type, Details, Documents,
  Pricing — 1–5. Pending-case intake is its shorter two-screen branch — 1–2.
- Client and advocate court lifecycle: Request Submitted, Under Review,
  Advocate Assigned, Client Decision, Documents, Case Preparation, Court Filing,
  First Hearing, Hearing Update, Next Hearing, Case Completed — eleven stages.
  Court progress lists and their numbered badges now use this sequence.
  Change-advocate review returns to Under Review; its separate three-action
  change-request checklist remains separate from the case timeline.
- High Court courier form labels explicitly say Delivery step 2/3 and 3/3;
  delivered-docket confirmation is step 3/4, matching its active row.
- KYC method choice is unnumbered; DigiLocker connect/check/result are 1/2/3.
  Video introduction is 1/3, capture 2/3, result 3/3. The device-check screen's
  requested pending copy is preserved, rather than silently edited.
Existing Legal Notice six-step intake, Property Search/Forensic three-step intake,
consistent service status lists, and separate fee/manual-verification checklists
retain their own totals.

6. SEVEN CLIENT IDENTITY CHECKPOINTS
- Legal Notice document upload, design 91: /legal-notice/step4
- Legal Notice document review, design 39: /legal-notice/step4-review
- High Court initial documents, design 294: /services/high-court/documents
- High Court requested documents, design 303: /services/high-court/documents-needed
- District Court initial documents, design 342: /services/district-court/documents
- District Court review, design 344: /services/district-court/review
- District Court requested documents, design 350: /services/district-court/documents-needed
Whichever checkpoint is entered first opens the existing KYC flow. Completing it
returns to that checkpoint. Later checkpoints show Identity verified, reuse the
result, and remove the repeated identity upload. Forward actions require that
result. Leaving verification without completing it does not mark it verified.
The result is session-scoped and clears on login/role changes; it is not persisted
across reloads, matching the app's current session model.

Genuine re-verification among these seven: none. No supplied process requirement
requires collecting the same client's Aadhaar again. Advocate enrollment's own ID,
Sanad/COP and missing-document submission remain: they concern a different person's
identity/professional credentials. Court physical-document receipt/manual checks
remain separate evidence/custody checks, not a new client Aadhaar upload.

Production limitation: the pre-existing DigiLocker/video implementation simulates
verification and has no identity-provider/backend integration. This work implements
reuse and routing, not real identity validation. Production verification still
requires a provider-issued, server-validated result; no provider credentials or
backend contract are present in this repository.
